From Beats to Volume
Stroke Volume

The Volume Behind Every Beat.

Stroke volume is the amount of blood that leaves a ventricle in one contraction.

It is simply the difference between how much blood is in the ventricle before contraction and how much remains after.

SV = EDV − ESV
0mL
End-Diastolic Volume Maximum ventricular volume before contraction.
0mL
End-Systolic Volume Blood remaining in the ventricle after contraction.
0mL
Stroke Volume Blood ejected with each heartbeat.
Clinical Reference
EF = SV / EDV × 100%
Normal ejection fraction: ≈ 55–70%
A key index of systolic function.
From Volume to Flow
Cardiac Output

One Minute of Circulation.

CO = HR × SV

Cardiac output is how much blood the heart pumps in one minute.

It depends on two things: how fast the heart beats, and how much blood leaves with each beat.

At rest, this is about 5 liters per minute — enough to keep blood circulating continuously.

During intense exercise, it can rise above 25 liters per minute, so oxygen delivery keeps up with the increased demand.

Rest5 L/min
Intense Exercise25 L/min

Blood Distribution

When Demand Changes, Flow Follows.

Blood flow is constantly redirected to wherever it is needed most.

At rest, much of it goes to the kidneys and gastrointestinal tract. During intense exercise, up to 85% can be redirected to skeletal muscle.

From Demand to Intrinsic Regulation
Frank-Starling Mechanism

The More It Fills,
the More It Pumps.

↑ EDV → ↑ Stretch → ↑ SV

As the ventricle fills more, the cardiac muscle fibers stretch further.

Within normal limits, more stretch means a stronger contraction — and a larger stroke volume.

This is intrinsic: the heart adjusts its own output to match how much blood returns to it.

Sympathetic stimulation strengthens contraction further, shifting the curve up and to the left.

Autonomic Regulation

Two Systems.
Opposite Effects.

Sympathetic Nervous System

Sympathetic activation increases the firing rate of the SA node. Threshold is reached sooner, so heart rate rises.

It also strengthens ventricular contraction.

Parasympathetic Nervous System

Parasympathetic activation slows spontaneous depolarization and pushes the membrane potential further from threshold.

Heart rate falls. Ventricular contraction is barely affected directly.
